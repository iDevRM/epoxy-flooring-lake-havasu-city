What Does Garage Floor Epoxy Involve?

Garage floor epoxy is more prep than paint. The concrete gets diamond ground with a vacuum-equipped grinder, cracks and pits are filled, then a base coat goes down and colored flakes are broadcast over it until the base is fully covered. After the loose flakes are scraped and vacuumed, a clear polyaspartic topcoat seals the floor.

Flake blends from makers like Torginol come in dozens of color mixes. Lighter blends hide the desert dust best, which matters when the door is open half the day.

One question comes up on almost every estimate: “Can you just roll it over the old paint?” No. The old paint comes off first, or the new floor fails with it.

When Do You Need Garage Floor Epoxy?

  • A DIY kit or old paint is lifting in tire tracks.
  • Oil, coolant or pool-chemical stains won’t scrub out.
  • The slab is dusting and tracking grit into the house.
  • You’re moving into a new build and want it done before the shelves go in.

Why Does Garage Floor Epoxy Peel?

Peeling almost always traces back to bond. A coating rolled onto smooth, sealed or dirty concrete is sitting on the surface, not gripping it. Add a tire that just came off a long drive in July, and the softened coating sticks to the rubber and pulls up. Moisture pushing up through the slab does the same thing from below.

We see it every season: a floor that looked great in March is flaking by August. Grinding to an open profile and checking for moisture first is the fix.

Epoxy vs. a DIY Kit: Which Do You Need?

A big-box kit is cheaper and fine for a light-use shed. It’s thin, it goes on etched or unground concrete, and it usually fails at the tire tracks. A professional system is several times thicker and bonded to ground concrete. If you park inside, store a boat or work on vehicles, the kit is a short-term fix.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a garage floor epoxy job take?

Most two-car garages take one to two days. Day one is grinding, crack repair and the base coat with flakes; the topcoat goes on the same day with a fast-curing system or the next morning with a standard one.

Can you epoxy a garage floor with cracks?

Yes. Cracks are ground out and filled with a repair material before the coating goes down. Hairline cracks disappear under the flakes; a crack that is still moving may show again over time, and we’ll point those out at the estimate.

Is a flake epoxy floor slippery?

A full flake floor has a light texture from the flakes, so it is less slick than a smooth coating. For extra grip near a door or a ramp, we can add a fine anti-slip grit to the topcoat.

Do I need to empty my garage?

Yes, the floor needs to be clear wall to wall. Most people move things to the driveway or a side yard for a day or two. We’ll give you a timeline so you know when you can move back in.
